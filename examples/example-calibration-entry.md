# Worked example — a calibration entry (the M2/community format)

*This is the system's real calibration entry #1 (June 2026), reformatted as the contribution template in `CONTRIBUTING.md`. It's the model for what you'd submit upstream — note that it contains no live opportunity data: the candidate it references was killed and archived.*

## Calibration entry

**Stage:** S2 (scoring rubric)

**Predicted:** the rubric (v2.0) treated two candidates in a heavily saturated market as top-decile opportunities — both scored ~90/100, verdict STRONG.

**Actual:** the market had five entrenched, well-funded incumbents; a new entrant's realistic position was last place. The saturation signal existed in the evidence but the rubric structurally couldn't express it — Competitive Wedge carried only 4 of 100 points, so proven pain + spend + reachability swamped it.

**Sample:** n=2 candidates, 1 market (below the n≥3 re-weight bar on its own — logged, then confirmed by an independent external review of the same evidence, which met the "unambiguous external fact" standard).

**Proposed edit:** Competitive Wedge weight 4 → 10; sharpen its anchor ("1–2 = saturated, entrenched incumbents, no wedge"); fund it by trimming Pain & Urgency 18 → 16 and Free Reachability 18 → 14 (both were maxing out for proven-but-crowded spaces and inflating totals). Weights still sum to 100.

**Evidence class:** process-level — two same-market candidates re-scored before/after; the re-weight *flipped their ranking* (the better-wedge candidate now led), i.e., the calibration changed the decision, which is the point.

**Context that matters:** e-commerce tooling category; mature market with consolidation activity (one incumbent had already been acquired); evidence was forum-scale engagement + competitor traction data.

## What happened next (the governance trail)

The edit was applied as rubric v2.1 with a changelog entry. A follow-up note was logged but **held**: "if future cycles keep advancing saturated me-too markets, escalate from a weight to a saturation kill-gate — but don't over-fit to one rehearsal." That escalation still waits for its n≥5. Later (v3.0, after an external review), the same underlying failure produced a structural rule through the proper channel: Wedge scores must cite a positioning teardown, and Wedge ≤ 3 makes the S5 switch test mandatory.

**Why this is a good contribution:** specific stage, falsifiable prediction, measured effect (a flipped ranking), honest sample size, an edit scoped to exactly what the evidence supports — and nothing in it reveals what the contributor was building.
