# Acquirer Intelligence (S20)

*Community-maintained. Every row is public-source-compiled; each should carry a source and a last-verified date. Buyer criteria drift constantly — row updates are the easiest first contribution (see CONTRIBUTING.md).*

*Living reference · June 23, 2026 · the data foundation for Skill S20. Refresh ongoing via `last30days` (what named buyers are saying, gaps, hiring signals).*

> Built by reverse-engineering recent sub-$10M acquisitions of AI / AI-built solo & tiny-team products (2024–2026), plus instructive outliers and failures. Most micro-deal terms are private; rows are confidence-rated. This file feeds Recipe #0 (seeding), the Scorecard (S2), and Exit Prep (S19).

## 1. The two buyer motives (pick one per product)

| | **Financial / cash-flow** | **Strategic / capability-gap** |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Aggregators, micro-PE, vertical roll-ups, marketplace operator-buyers, search funds | AI incumbents & frontier labs |
| Buys | durable cash flow | a named capability + the team |
| Requires | **profit, recurring revenue, low churn, owner-independence** | being directly adjacent to the buyer's roadmap gap |
| Profit | gating | de-weighted (buys near-zero-revenue teams) |
| Multiple | **2–4x profit / ~3–5x ARR** (sub-$1M ARR) | priced on talent/IP scarcity; dispersed, premium |
| Predictability | **High — our base case** | Low — upside only |
| Speed | needs ~$250k+ ARR, owner-independent | can be fast when the adjacency is hot (Base44 ~6 mo) |
| Binding constraint | **operator/CEO talent** | **AI talent scarcity** |

**Strategy:** engineer primarily for the financial path; *bias seeds* toward strategically-adjacent spaces so the upside stays free. Do **not** design around an $80M lightning strike (Base44→Wix is the cleanest proof but an outlier on price, growth, and profitability).

## 2. Target-acquirer list (named, actionable)

> **⚠️ Reality flags (External Review #01, July 2026) — read before seeding against this list.**
> (1) **Minimums vs. our profile:** most named buyers below have floors ABOVE a 6-month-old product at $15–20k MRR — Tiny wants $500k–15M *profit* + 3+ yrs; saas.group $2–10M ARR; SureSwift $1–5M ARR; Constellation ~$1M EBIT; Valsoft $2–20M rev; Banyan >$2M; **even XO Capital's floor ($30–60k MRR) exceeds our exit trigger**. For a young sub-$500k-ARR asset the realistic venue is **marketplace operator-buyers** (Acquire/Flippa), who price on **TTM profit (~2–4x)**.
> (2) **TTM, not run-rate:** a product launched mid-year has months, not years, of trailing profit — $21k MRR reached by December ≈ $50–60k TTM ≈ a $200–500k asset. The $1M-class sale matures with ~6 months of cohort-retention proof (~mid-2027) or an outlier strategic.
> (3) Use this list two ways: *seeding adjacency* (unchanged — build in spaces these buyers roll up) and *exit sequencing* (new — know which buyer becomes reachable at which MRR/age milestone, and don't pitch above your trailing numbers).
> (4) **This list is a DIRECTORY, not a per-deal answer (owner directive, July 2026).** Every candidate needs an **opportunity-specific buyer map**: each named buyer justified by a *specific portfolio asset or deal* evidencing appetite for that exact asset class, classified as **thesis-buyer** (portfolio-adjacent, cited) / **size-class buyer** (buys our shape at some scale, no thesis fit) / **plan-of-record venue** (Acquire/Flippa on TTM profit), with the milestone at which each becomes reachable. S0 requires the citation; S4 validates the map for finalists; a famous name without portfolio adjacency counts as *no named buyer* in Acquirability.
> *Worked example (archived candidate — Shopify payout reconciliation, killed pre-build):* Visma = **thesis-buyer** for that space via its Link My Books acquisition (e-commerce accounting automation); generic aggregators = **size-class only** (no e-comm-accounting portfolio asset); plan-of-record venue = Acquire/Flippa at TTM profit. The candidate was killed at the demand gate — but the buyer map shows the method: every name cites a portfolio asset, or it doesn't count.

### Buy-and-hold aggregators / micro-PE
- **Tiny** (Wilkinson): simple/profitable/"boring"; **$500k–$15M/yr profit**; 3+ yrs, moat = brand/community/niche; offer in ~7 days. Constraint they name: operator talent. *Conf: High.*
- **saas.group**: B2B SaaS only, bootstrapped/PLG; **$2–10M ARR** (flexes lower), ~2–3x ARR; Rule of 40/50, NDR ~80%+. Explicitly wants to "become an AI player"; hiring Head of AI / Origination. *High.*
- **SureSwift Capital**: bootstrapped B2B SaaS; **$1–5M ARR**, ~3–5x TTM profit; ~50% margins, low churn; hold forever. *High/Med.*
- **WebStreet** (ex-EF Capital): PM-operated micro-PE; **~$0.5–1.5M/asset**, 1.5–2x ARR upfront (cap ~3.5x w/ earnouts); gate = operator track record. *High.*
- **Dura Software**: hyper-niche mission-critical B2B; **$2–10M ARR**; gate = sustained Rule of 40, 30% IRR. *High.*
- **XO Capital** (Andrew Pierno): single-purpose B2B SaaS; **$30–60k MRR**, 2–5x SDE; 80%+ GM, 50+ customers, PLG; full founder exit ~30 days. *High.*
- Also: **Awesome Motive** (WordPress/plugins, $250k–$10M rev), **Relay Commerce** (e-com tools ≥$1M ARR), **HOLD.co**, **Brydon Group** ($1–5M EBITDA).

### Vertical-SaaS roll-ups
- **Constellation Software** + operating groups (**Volaris, Harris, Topicus, Perseus, Vela, Jonas**): VMS, mission-critical, never sells; avg deal **$2–4M**; min ~$1M EBIT, ~20%+ growth, high recurring, low attrition. **Openly short of quality small targets vs capital.** Volaris has live M&A-analyst job postings (inbound is read). *High.*
- **Valsoft**: mission-critical VMS, founder-operated; **$2–20M rev (sweet spot $5–10M)**; most active strategic software buyer 2 yrs running (16 deals in 2025). *Med-High.*
- **Banyan Software**: niche-leading enterprise software; **>$2M revenue**; profitable, high recurring, high retention; closes 45–60 days. *High.*
- EU: **Everfield, Main Capital, Vitec, Lifco** (Lifco's #1 criterion: EBIT margin ≥20%).

### Marketplaces / operator-buyers (the realistic exit venue for us)
- **Acquire.com** (Gazdecki): sub-$50M; median **3.9x SDE** (2024 & 2025). **Buyers want workflow replacement / security / execution infra / enterprise control — "API wrappers are invisible."** *High/Med.*
- **Flippa**: high volume; **<$1M ARR sells on profit (2–4x)**; ARR multiples only kick in with growth (20–40% → 5–7x). Wants churn <6%, GM>75%, <15 owner-hrs/wk. *High.*
- **Empire Flippers**: curated; **$50k–$10M+**; SaaS ~3.3–4.2x annual profit; rewards operational independence. *Med.*
- **FE International**: brokerage; SDE **4–10x** (<$2M val → 5–7x; >$2M → 7–10x). *Med.*
- **Search funds/ETA:** mostly moving *away* from software; for sub-$10M software the real buyers are self-funded searchers + operator funds (WebStreet), not classic funded searchers.

### Strategic capability-gap buyers (highest-multiple, team-led)
Active, named gaps — map a product's one-liner to one of these:
- **Agents/execution:** Salesforce (most prolific; ~1,000 forward-deployed hires for Agentforce), ServiceNow (Moveworks $2.85B), UiPath, Asana (StackAI $75M — first acquisition in 18 yrs), Workday (Sana ~$1.1B).
- **AI security (deep pockets):** Palo Alto, CrowdStrike ("agentic SOC"), Cisco, SentinelOne, Check Point, Zscaler, F5, Okta — buying red-team/runtime/posture teams (~$100–400M, near-zero revenue, team is the asset).
- **Dev tools:** Cursor/Anysphere, Vercel, Replit (talent acqui-hires).
- **Gen media / productivity:** Canva (Leonardo.ai), Figma (Weavy ~$200M), Notion, Dropbox, Box, Shopify.
- Reverse acqui-hires (license + hire, leave a shell): MSFT/Inflection, Google/Character.AI & Windsurf founders, Meta/Scale.

## 3. Buying-criteria checklist

| Dimension | Financial buyer | Strategic buyer |
|---|---|---|
| Profitability | **Required (gating)** | de-weighted |
| Valuation basis | profit/SDE (2–7x); ARR multiples only $5M+/high-growth | talent + strategic value (multiples of last raise) |
| Revenue quality | recurring, low concentration, churn <2–6%/mo, NRR ≥110% | often pilots/zero; IP & data moat matter |
| Switching cost | mission-criticality, niche dominance, brand/community | proprietary algos/patents, exclusive data |
| Owner-independence | **critical** (outsourced ops = +0.5–0.75x; "buyers don't want a job") | inverse — they want the founders |
| Team | replaceable | the whole point (5–40 people) |
| AI stance | wary of wrappers (margin/churn risk) | premium — but reject undifferentiated wrappers |

## 4. How to position a $1–10M asset

- **Financial (base case):** maximize *boring durability* — 40–50%+ margins, demonstrably low churn, recurring & diversified revenue, **runs without you on day one** (SOPs, replaceable team, no key-person/customer concentration), a defensible niche moat. Rule of 40 + ~50% margins fits saas.group/SureSwift/Dura/Valsoft at once.
- **Vertical roll-up:** be the proprietary #1/#2 in a narrow vertical with >$1M EBIT, ~20% growth, high recurring, low attrition, clean cap table.
- **Marketplace listing:** clean books with SDE bridged, MRR over plans, churn <6%, <15 owner-hrs/wk, transferable stack/payments, documented code. Avoid being an undifferentiated AI wrapper.
- **Strategic (upside):** don't optimize profit — be the tight team (5–30) that closes a *named* gap (agents, AI security, gen media), with hard/defensible IP, an exclusive data asset, and 1–2 lighthouse customers. Map your one-liner to a CEO's stated gap. Expect acqui-hire economics + retention terms.

## 5. Hard truths (filters to bake in)
1. **No undifferentiated AI wrappers** — invisible to buyers. Need workflow ownership, a data moat, or real switching cost. → new Scorecard kill gate.
2. **Owner-independence is the financial-buyer gate** — design for transferability from day one. → new maxim + Scorecard sub-signal.
3. **Two-tier valuation** — small assets sell on profit/SDE (~3–7x), not AI fundraising multiples. Keep exit math honest: a $1M sale needs ~$250k ARR; the strategic premium is a bonus, not the plan.
4. **Match a buyer before building** — a known, active acquirer adjacent to the space raises odds. → new seeding lens + maxim.
